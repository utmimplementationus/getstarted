Drone as First Responder & Public Safety UAS

How life-safety missions, like Drone as First Responder (DFR) programs, fit into the US Shared Airspace UTM Implementation.

In this cohort's context

What is DFR / public safety UAS?

Drone as First Responder (DFR) programs let public safety agencies, police, fire, EMS, and emergency management, launch a drone ahead of or alongside a traditional response, often flying BVLOS from a fixed site to a call location.

Public safety UAS more broadly covers any mission flown by or on behalf of a public safety agency: active search-and-rescue, disaster response, and time-critical delivery of items like AEDs or Narcan, in addition to DFR.

These operators fly using the same shared airspace network as commercial operators in the cohort, so their flight intents need to be visible to, and deconflicted with, everyone else flying nearby.

Why it's called out separately

Life-safety missions need to move fast

DFR and public safety missions are frequently unplanned and time-critical: a 911 call comes in, and the drone needs to be airborne in seconds. That's different from the pre-planned missions most commercial operators fly, and it's why the cohort treats these missions as a distinct, high-priority category.

DFR missions are one of the categories explicitly named in the cohort's Operational Prioritization Policy as eligible for high priority.
Shared airspace & UTM coordination

How DFR fits into the network

Public safety UAS operators use the same standards-based coordination as every other member of the cohort, with one key difference: how they're prioritized when flight paths conflict.

High-priority coordination

When a qualifying DFR or public safety flight overlaps another operation, providers notify the affected operator per the ASTM standard, and accepted, standard-priority flights must re-plan around the life-safety mission.

Read the prioritization policy

Qualifying to fly high priority

An operator can't self-declare high priority. Qualifying evidence includes an SGI waiver, a Certificate of Waiver or Authorization (COA), or a DFR Certificate of Waiver, submitted to the Operations Committee.

See the onboarding path

Outside of prioritization, DFR and public safety operators onboard the same way as any other operator: partnering with a UTM service provider, signing the flowed-down governance requirements, and proving operational practicality through simulation. See How to Join for the full path.
