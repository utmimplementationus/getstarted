The journey, step by step

What happens, and in what order

There are two starting steps, then three "gates" you pass in sequence. Each gate is about showing your system works, first on its own, then with others, then in production.

1
Get started

Sign the Governance Agreement

Email the Operations Committee with the name and email of the person who'll sign. They send you the Form of Joinder (Annex E) via DocuSign. Then share your committee points of contact and GitHub handles.

  • Send your signing contact to the Operations Committee
  • Sign the Form of Joinder (Annex E)
  • Provide your Operations & Technical Committee POCs + Accountable Executive

Unlocks observer status in both committees while you onboard.

2
Prepare

Get set up for automated testing

Once you sign, your 120-day clock starts. You get a test configuration so you can run tests independently, without affecting live participants. You'll gather artifacts like proof of insurance, security/privacy/quality attestations (or ISO/IEC certs), and an operator relationship (SLA signature page).

You can actually pass Gate 1 with your own tooling before signing, using the open-source InterUSS test suite. Signing unlocks the shared "RPAS Platform" test environments.
G1
Gate 1 · Qualification Testing

Prove your own system works

Implement the service requirements from the applicable Service Description Document, then pass automated testing on your own system.

  • Own-system passing test result
  • DSS contract signature page (if applicable)
  • On-call contact for SLA notifications
  • Declaration of a completed safety hazard assessment
G2
Gate 2 · Simulation

Prove you work with everyone else

Pass automated testing across the ecosystem, and integrate your systems with your operator so you can exchange data the way the standard requires.

  • Early Integration Assessment (ecosystem) passing result
  • Service provider & operator declaration of integration
  • Request inclusion in the Simulation Fitness test configuration

Note: "Integration" means the technical ability to exchange data. It does not require FAA approvals, which run on their own timeline.

G3
Gate 3 · Production Flights

Prove operational practicality, then go live

Operators run coordinated, simultaneous simulations with the other members they expect to overlap with in real life, establishing "operational practicality." Service providers pass Production Fitness testing as part of their release process.

  • Operators: shared-airspace simulation + KML of flight volumes + aggregate conformance report + affirmation from each operator
  • Providers: Production Fitness (ecosystem) passing result
  • Operations Committee confirms successful onboarding
You're in

Certified & cleared for production

You receive a certificate from the Co-Chairs and can request access to the production environment. Voting rights are granted after Gate 3. Keep meeting the ongoing requirements to stay active. Welcome to the shared airspace.

Already working with a service provider that's live on the network?

1
Get started

Partner with an onboarded service provider

Parter with a service provider that has already completed onboarding and is live on the network. They provision your UTM services and connect your operations to the shared airspace.

2
Paperwork

Sign service provider's SLA; governance flows down to you

Sign a Service Level Agreement with your provider. The SLA flows the cohort's governance requirements down to you as the operator, in addition to provider specific terms & conditions. You, or your provider on your behalf, submit the signed signature page as evidence.

  • Signed SLA between you and your service provider
  • SLA signature page submitted (by you or your USP)
3
Operational practicality

Prove practicality through simulation

With your service provider, run coordinated, simultaneous shared-airspace simulation with the overlapping operators you reasonably expect to encounter, to establish operational practicality.

  • Shared-airspace simulation with overlapping members
  • KML of operational intent volumes
  • Aggregate conformance report
  • Affirmation from each operator
4
Optional

Request high-priority operations

If you'll fly time-critical, life-safety missions, submit a request to use high priority, along with the required eligibility evidence.

See the prioritization policy

You're in

Cleared to operate in shared airspace

You're ready to fly alongside other members in shared airspace, with your provider handling the UTM service connection.

Quick reference

The three gates at a glance

Gate 1

Qualification Testing

Your system, on its own, meets the requirements and passes automated testing.

Gate 2

Simulation

Your system passes ecosystem testing and integrates with your operator.

Gate 3

Production Flights

Operational practicality is proven through simulation; production testing passes; onboarding is confirmed.

Timelines & extensions

After signing, you have a defined onboarding window to complete all gates and submit your artifacts. Miss it, and you're removed from the implementation and lose observer access. But you can request a one-time 30-day extension before the window closes, no conditions attached.

Removed applicants may reapply after a 90-day waiting period.

Already onboarded for one service?

The cohort runs two services today: Strategic Coordination (SCD) and Network Remote ID (NRID). If you've onboarded for one, the second has a streamlined path that builds on your existing Gate 0 status.
